Today I'd like to talk to you about finding a voice. The voice of a character, our own
voice and the voice of God. Ever since the pandemic began, I've been reading the original
Marvel comic books to my son. Fantastic Four, Spider-Man, Thor, Iron Man, The Avengers, Doctor
Strange, Daredevil. If there's an online collection of them for free from the library, we've
been reading it. Each hero, even the villains, have their own peculiar voice.
Captain America has a thread of iron in his voice. He's disciplined, sharp, focused. When
Captain America throws his mighty shield, you better believe that those who oppose his
shield must yield. Tony Stark, aka Iron Man, is cool, confident, suave. His heart might
give out on him at any moment, but otherwise, he's totally in control. The Everloving Thing
talks rough. If you're the Everloving Thing, maybe expectations ain't so high to be all
refined like. Reed Richards is serious, a little on edge, and knows that he's right.
It isn't arrogance if it's true. Johnny Storm is still going through puberty, even though
he's practically in college. Peter Parker wishes he was as cool as Tony Stark, but at
least he made it through puberty. Matt Murdock, aka Daredevil, has the voice that'll stop
a judge and a thief in their tracks. Ant-Man talks like he's been shrunk down to the
size of an insect, even if he's as big as a house. And Thor speaks in a declamatory
fashion fitting for a god. Knowing who each character is helps me in reading their lines.
For my son, the adventure is the story. For me, the adventure is capturing the moment
with my voice. So, if I know the voice of Captain America versus the voice of Red Skull,
the voice of Thor versus the voice of Loki, it should stand to reason that I should know
my own voice. And yet I know I need to work on my own voice. What voice really is mine?
There's a tone of voice I sometimes have that comes up when I'm exasperated and frustrated.
My wife and children have regrettably heard that one, and it shuts them down. That's
not the voice I want. There's a tone of voice when I'm hesitating to say what I know
is right. And I leave things sort of as questions. I don't want that to be my voice either. There's
a tone of voice I have when I'm distracted and not really keeping track of what I'm
saying because I'm somewhere else mentally, usually looking at my phone. Gosh, I don't
want to go through life not where I am. That can't be my voice. Where then do I look
for my own voice? When am I authentically speaking? I would like to offer that the
best way that I have found to find my own voice is and always has been in relation
to the voice of God. Jesus said that his sheep know and hear his voice. God calls us by name.
So we should be able to hear him. In a document called Godium et spes, the fathers of the
Second Vatican Council wrote, Christ the final Adam, by the revelation of the mystery of the
father and his love, fully reveals man to himself and makes his supreme calling clear.
They wrote Christ fully reveals man to man himself. If I can hear his voice, not only
is he revealing himself to me, he also is revealing me to me. When I'm listening to
God, my ensuing speech has at least three characteristics it otherwise lacks. First,
my speech is free to be vulnerable. I don't need to be on the defensive nor on the offensive.
Second, my speech is patterned on truth. I'm built for imitation and there's no more honest
speaker than the word of God. Third, my speech is laser focused on the present moment. The
present moment is literally a present from God and my speech reflects that. So I'd like to leave
you with a twofold call to action. The first, if you have anyone in your life that you can read
out loud to, do it. Have fun experimenting with voices, with accents, with styles, with attitudes,
just as an aside, all of the cops in all of the Marvel comic books so far, they talk with an
Irish accent. I don't know why, but it's what comes out. Silly things like that can be part of your
life too. The second call to action, to listen for a bit before you speak. Don't just listen to
the people around you, important though they are. Take a moment and listen for the voice of God.
We're much clearer speakers when we're responders to the divine. From our outpost to yours,
