Ancient fabulous Esop said, a man is known by the company
he keeps. I think we are known also by the books we've read. In this episode, I'd like to convince
you all of the importance of reading. This is not a trivial matter. This is one of the ways that we
shape ourselves into who we want to be. And who are we? We are human beings and a distinctly human
craft, as it were, is in the arranging of stories and ideas for maximum effect. Should we not then
seek out the best crafters of language, living or dead, and learn from them, when we read, we become
an apprentice to that author. If we've chosen well, we'll learn not only more content to say,
but also how to say it. Their thoughts shape our thoughts. Their words shape our words.
When I read Shakespeare, I sit at the foot of a master linguist and storyteller.
My tongue becomes inflamed with living language. Imagery peppers my sentences.
I pass off neologisms as if they're already cliche. I reach new heights standing on his
shoulders. Conversely, if I refrain from reading, my sentences become clipped. My mind struggles for
words. I lose the flow because I've stepped out of the river of the word. Reading shapes my very
brain. If I fall into only reading 140 characters at a time and stick to headlines and soundbites
and clips, I condition myself to think in short little spurts. If I commit to reading longer stories
and longer non-fiction works, through effort I become capable of increased attention and
longer retention. My dear listeners, I am less myself when I'm not reading, and more present
to stories and ideas when I make it a practice to read, and the more present to stories and
ideas I am, the more I am attentive to the very narrative of my own life. One of my favorite
questions is to ask people what they've been reading recently. One of my friends would be
at a breakfast with me each week a few years back, and I would always ask her what she'd been
reading recently. It took about a month of, I don't really read, for her to pick up a book
so she would have an answer. She went on to thank me years later for helping her pick up the habit
of reading. She became more herself in the process. Now clearly, there are good books
and bad books. Some books shape us well, and others rot our minds, our hearts, or our souls.
I recommend the good books. If it's been a while, I invite you to dust off one of your old
friends, pick up a well-loved tome, and become reacquainted. Let it introduce you to some of its
friends and neighbors, and become the man or woman you choose to be by the literary company you keep.
